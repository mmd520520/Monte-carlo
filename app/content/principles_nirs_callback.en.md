#### Looking back: our earlier near-infrared (NIRS) research

On 2026-10-22 the optics team gave a Share & Learn talk: **"Near-infrared (NIRS) brain monitoring: a summary and where it can go"**. It laid out the whole chain of "seeing the brain with near-infrared light":

- which physiological signals to measure;
- why 700–900 nm;
- how light travels through scalp, skull, cerebrospinal fluid and cortex;
- what continuous-wave, frequency-domain and time-domain NIRS each measure;
- how detector readings are turned back into blood oxygenation and blood flow.

It also made clear where Monte Carlo fits: **the most accurate forward model**, used to compute path lengths and depth sensitivity that the inversion relies on. Most of what this engine adds next comes from that talk. The slides are in the team's internal materials.
